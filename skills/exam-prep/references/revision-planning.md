# Revision planning

Depth for CLI agents. The StudyHub app loads `SKILL.md` only, so nothing here
may be load-bearing — the skill must work correctly if this file is never read.

## Working backwards from an exam date

Split the days remaining into three unequal phases. The proportions matter more
than the exact days:

| Phase | Share of the time | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage | ~50% | Every module touched once, weakest first. |
| Consolidation | ~35% | Only the topics that were shaky. Timed questions. |
| Rehearsal | ~15% | Whole papers under exam conditions, no notes. |

Weakest-first ordering is the point. A student who revises in module order
spends their freshest days on module 1, which they already know, and meets
module 5 the night before.

## Sizing a session

- A topic with fewer than ~8 questions in the bank cannot fill a session on its
  own. Pair it with a neighbouring topic from the same module.
- Marks are a better time estimate than question count: roughly one minute per
  mark, plus a third again for marking and discussion.
- Stop a session at three consecutive correct answers on a topic. It is secure;
  further questions on it buy nothing.

## Spacing revisits

A topic answered poorly comes back after 1 day, then 3, then 7. If it fails on a
revisit, the interval resets rather than shortening — the failure means the
method was never learned, so re-teach before re-testing.

## What a diagnosis should say

Not "you scored 62%". Three lists:

1. **Secure** — answered correctly without hints, twice.
2. **Shaky** — correct only with a hint, or correct once and wrong once.
3. **Not started** — topics in the outline the session never reached.

The third list is the one students forget to make for themselves, and it is
usually where the marks are lost.
